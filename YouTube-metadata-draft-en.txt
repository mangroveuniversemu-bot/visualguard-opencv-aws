TITLE
VisualGuard | Two Views. One Next Step. (OpenCV 5 + AWS)

DESCRIPTION
One camera angle can miss a warning on the other side of a machine. VisualGuard explores a clearer next step for equipment-panel inspection by combining front and side views with a human-reviewed workflow.

In this 2-minute, 20-second demonstration, see how the prototype recommends REVIEW when evidence is clear, STOP when a side-view warning appears, and REQUEST_VIEW when an image is obstructed. An operator can provide a replacement view and inspect the result again.

HOW IT WORKS
The Python application uses OpenCV 5 to assess image quality, find red indicator candidates, and combine two views into a recommended next step. The same inspection core runs locally and in an AWS Lambda container. Recommendations leave the final decision with a person; the prototype does not send commands to equipment.

WHAT WE MEASURED
Across 540 held-out synthetic image pairs from 18 scenario families, exact decision agreement was 94.26%, compared with 50.00% for a simple red-count baseline. This is agreement on the synthetic scenarios, not real-world accuracy.

Known limits: all 30 round red-sticker cases triggered a false STOP, and one motion-blurred synthetic case returned REVIEW instead of REQUEST_VIEW. In a local exploration of 15 archived exhibition images, no red candidates were returned. Those images have no labelled machine-alarm ground truth, so alarm accuracy was not measured.

The four saved AWS invocations used health, synthetic, or empty-view inputs. No live camera was connected and no equipment commands were issued.
